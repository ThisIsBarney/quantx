Quick start¶

Ground states¶

Build a model, then solve it in a symmetry sector:

from quantx_engine.models import HubbardChain, XXZChain
from quantx_engine.dmrg import ground_state

gs = ground_state(HubbardChain(L=16, U=4.0), n_up=8, n_dn=8, max_bond=256)
gs.energy, gs.truncation_error, gs.sector

gs = ground_state(XXZChain(L=21, delta=0.5), sz=0.5)   # an odd chain, S^z = 1/2

The models are XXZChain, J1J2Chain, Spin1Chain, HubbardChain and HubbardLattice (ladders, strips and cylinders).

Fermionic circuits¶

from quantx_engine.models import HubbardLattice
from quantx_engine import hva

cyl = HubbardLattice(Lx=4, W=3, U=4.0, y_periodic=True)
angles = [[0.1] * hva.layout(cyl).angles_per_layer] * 3      # 3 layers
r = hva.circuit(cyl, angles, n_up=6, n_dn=6, max_bond=256)   # ~10 s
r.energy, r.entropy_max, r.truncation_error, r.init_energy_error

This cylinder already truncates at max_bond=256: truncation_error is about 5e-4, and the energy moves by 3e-3 at max_bond=1024. Raise max_bond until it stops moving.

Finite temperature¶

from quantx_engine import thermal
from quantx_engine.models import XXZChain

sf = thermal.structure_factor(XXZChain(L=12), beta=1.0, t_max=4.0)   # ~10 s
k, omega, S = sf.spectrum()     # S(k, omega, T), both signs of omega

The ensemble is grand canonical. quantx_engine.thermal also gives the statics and the optical conductivity.

All three, checked¶

examples/quickstart.py in the repository runs a Hubbard ground state, a finite-temperature spectral function and an HVA circuit, and asserts each against an exact answer computed without the engine. CI runs it on every wheel it builds.

from quantx_engine import hva, thermal
from quantx_engine.dmrg import ground_state
from quantx_engine.models import HubbardChain, HubbardLattice, XXZChain

# A Hubbard ground state: 8 sites, U = 4, 4 up and 4 down electrons.
gs = ground_state(HubbardChain(L=8, U=4.0), n_up=4, n_dn=4)
print(f"Hubbard ground-state energy  {gs.energy:.10f}")
assert abs(gs.energy - -4.2358069991) < 1e-9            # exact diagonalisation

# A finite-temperature spectral function: S(k, omega, T) of the Heisenberg chain at T = 1.
sf = thermal.structure_factor(XXZChain(L=8), beta=1.0, t_max=4.0, max_bond=256)
k, omega, S = sf.spectrum()
print(f"Peak of S(k, omega, T)       {S.max():.6f}")
assert abs(S.max() - 0.795005) < 1e-3                    # exact diagonalisation

# An HVA circuit: two layers on a 3 x 2 Hubbard ladder, from a product state.
ladder = HubbardLattice(Lx=3, W=2, U=4.0)
angles = [[0.1] * hva.layout(ladder).angles_per_layer] * 2
r = hva.circuit(ladder, angles, n_up=3, n_dn=3, init="product")
print(f"HVA circuit energy           {r.energy:.10f}")
assert abs(r.energy - -0.3501424393) < 1e-9              # state vector

Conventions¶

  • The spin models use physical spin operators S = σ/2 throughout.

  • Every chain is open.

  • Hubbard is H = −t Σ (c†c + h.c.) + U Σ n↑n↓ − μ Σ n.

  • A solver works in one symmetry sector: total Sz, or the numbers of up and down electrons.

  • An unreachable sector, or an unknown argument, is an error rather than a silent default.

Reading the results¶

  • truncation_error is the weight the bond-dimension cap discarded. For ground_state it is summed over the last sweep; for hva.circuit it is the largest single truncation.

  • An HVA circuit started from the U = 0 ground state (init="free", the default) inherits that state’s DMRG error. init_energy_error is the start’s energy against the exact U = 0 energy. A large value flags a bad start, but a small one does not bound the circuit’s error: the energy is stationary at the ground state, so a start right to 1e-14 in energy can still carry ~1e-8 into the circuit. For the full precision, start from init="product".
