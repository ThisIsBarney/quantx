Advanced Physics¶

Specialised solvers for topological phases, driven quantum systems, and exotic hardware platforms.

Floquet Systems¶

Periodic driving, quasi-energy spectrum, Floquet Hamiltonian.

Floquet engineering and Bloch-Floquet band structure — M92.

Provides:

  • FloquetSystem — defines a periodically driven Hamiltonian H(t) = H_static + H_drive · f(t) and computes the stroboscopic one-period propagator U(T) = T-exp(−i ∫₀ᵀ H(t) dt) via Runge-Kutta.

  • BlochFloquetSolver — diagonalises U(T) to extract quasi-energies (Floquet eigenvalues) and Floquet modes; computes effective Hamiltonians via the Floquet-Magnus expansion to second order; evaluates the micromotion operator U_M(t) = U(t, 0) · e^{+i H_eff t}.

  • floquet_spectrum() — convenience wrapper returning a FloquetResult for a given system.

  • floquet_effective_hamiltonian() — first-order Magnus approximation H_eff = H₀ + [H₁, H₋₁] / (2ω) for a single harmonic drive.

All matrix operations use dense NumPy arrays (suitable for small systems, e.g. 1–4 qubits / spins).

References

[1]

Eckardt, A. (2017). Colloquium: Atomic quantum gases in periodically driven optical lattices. Rev. Mod. Phys. 89, 011004. https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.89.011004

[2]

Kitagawa, T. et al. (2010). Exploring topological phases with quantum walks. Phys. Rev. A 82, 033429. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.82.033429

[3]

Shirley, J. H. (1965). Solution of the Schroedinger equation with a Hamiltonian periodic in time. Phys. Rev. 138, B979. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.138.B979

Physical validation¶

Quasi-energies come in +/- symmetric pairs due to time-reversal symmetry of H(t)=H(-t) (symmetric Hamiltonians). Effective Hamiltonian via matrix logarithm: H_eff = i*log(U_F)/T where U_F is the one-period propagator.

Non-Equilibrium Dynamics¶

Kibble-Zurek mechanism, quantum quenches, work statistics.

M114: Non-equilibrium quantum dynamics.

Three complementary tools for studying systems far from equilibrium:

Keldysh Green’s functions (KeldyshSolver)

Computes the single-particle retarded Green’s function G^R(t) = −iθ(t)⟨{c(t), c†(0)}⟩ and the spectral function A(ω) = −(1/π) Im G^R(ω + iη) for a quadratic Hamiltonian.

Entanglement dynamics (EntanglementDynamics)

Tracks bipartite von Neumann entropy S_A(t) = −Tr[ρ_A ln ρ_A] and mutual information I(A:B) during exact real-time evolution of an n-qubit state.

Kibble-Zurek scaling (KibbleZurekScaling)

Measures defect density after quenching through the critical point of the 1D transverse-field Ising model at different ramp rates and fits the power-law exponent predicted by the Kibble-Zurek mechanism.

Physical validation¶

Kibble-Zurek mechanism for 1D TFIM: defect density xi ~ tau_Q^{-nu/(1+nu*z)} For 1D TFIM: nu=1, z=1 => xi ~ tau_Q^{-0.5}. Power law exponent = -0.5. Verified by linear regression on log(xi) vs log(tau_Q) in test suite.

Rydberg Atom Arrays (M121)¶

Rydberg blockade Hamiltonian, 2D atom array, phase diagram simulation.

M118: Neutral-atom backend — Rydberg blockade simulator.

Simulates neutral-atom quantum processors (QuEra Aquila, Pasqal) based on the Rydberg blockade mechanism. Atoms are modelled as two-level systems |g⟩ (ground) and |r⟩ (Rydberg), driven by a global laser field.

Physics model¶

The many-body Hamiltonian (in units where ħ = 1) is:

H(t) = (Ω/2) Σ_i σ^x_i  −  δ Σ_i n_i  +  Σ_{i<j} V_{ij} n_i n_j

where:

  • Ω — Rabi frequency of the global drive

  • δ — laser detuning

  • n_i = |r⟩⟨r|_i — Rydberg population on atom i

  • V_{ij} = C₆ / r_{ij}⁶ — van der Waals blockade interaction (C₆ is the Rydberg C₆ coefficient, r_{ij} the inter-atom distance)

For r_{ij} < R_b (blockade radius R_b = (C₆/Ω)^{1/6}), two atoms cannot simultaneously occupy the Rydberg state.

Usage¶

atoms = [(0.0, 0.0), (5.0, 0.0), (10.0, 0.0)]  # μm
sim = RydbergSimulator(atom_positions=atoms, Omega=1.0, delta=0.0, C6=862690)
result = sim.evolve(t_final=2*pi, n_steps=100)
# result.populations[i] = Rydberg population ⟨n_i⟩(t)

Physical validation¶

Van der Waals blockade: V(r) = C_6 / r^6. Blockade radius r_b defined by V(r_b) = Omega (Rabi frequency). For r < r_b, doubly-excited state is energetically suppressed. Rabi oscillations: Omega_eff = sqrt(n)*Omega for an n-atom ensemble in the blockade regime [Lukin2001].
