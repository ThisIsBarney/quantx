quantx_engine.thermal¶

Finite-temperature statics and dynamics of the spin and Hubbard chains.

The Gibbs state rho = e^{-beta H} / Z of an open chain is held on the native engine by ancilla purification. Each physical site is paired with an ancilla, and the pure state e^{-beta H/2}|I> on the doubled chain (|I> is the infinite-temperature state) is reached by imaginary-time block TEBD. Its real-time evolution gives the dynamical correlations. Three quantities are exposed:

  • statics(): the energy, local magnetization or density, correlations and the uniform susceptibility over a range of inverse temperatures;

  • structure_factor(): the spin correlations C_j(t) = <S^z_j(t) S^z_c(0)>_beta, whose Fourier transform is the dynamical structure factor S(k, omega, T);

  • current_correlation(): the current-current correlation <J(t) J(0)>_beta, whose Fourier transform gives the optical conductivity sigma(omega, T).

Example:

from quantx_engine import thermal
from quantx_engine.models import HubbardChain, XXZChain

st = thermal.statics(XXZChain(L=16), beta=2.0)
st.energy[-1]                          # <H> at beta = 2

sf = thermal.structure_factor(XXZChain(L=16), beta=1.0, t_max=6.0)
k, w, S = sf.spectrum()                # S(k, omega, T)

cc = thermal.current_correlation(HubbardChain(L=12, U=4.0, mu=2.0), beta=1.0, t_max=6.0)
w, sigma = cc.conductivity()           # Re sigma(omega, T)

Conventions are those of quantx_engine.models (spins S = sigma/2). The ensemble is grand canonical: every S^z sector, and for Hubbard every particle number, with mu setting the filling (mu = U/2 is half filling at every temperature).

The models are XXZChain, Spin1Chain and HubbardChain. J1J2Chain and the Hubbard lattices have no purified form in the engine yet, and are refused.

Everything here is gated against exact diagonalisation of the full Hilbert space (tests/test_thermal_ed.py), with the second-order Trotter error of the steps dbeta and dt as the tolerance.

References

  • Verstraete, Garcia-Ripoll and Cirac (2004). Phys. Rev. Lett. 93, 207204. DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevLett.93.207204 (ancilla purification)

  • Karrasch, Bardarson and Moore (2012). Phys. Rev. Lett. 108, 227206. DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevLett.108.227206 (the backward ancilla evolution, disentangle)

spectrum(*, n_omega=401, omega_max=None, eta=None)[source]¶

The dynamical structure factor S(k, omega, T).

S(k, omega) = sum_r e^{-i k r} int dt e^{i omega t} C_{c+r}(t), on the open chain (r measured from the source), with C(-t) = conj(C(t)) for the negative times and a Gaussian time window exp(-(eta t)^2 / 2) against the cut-off at the last time. That extension is exact for the local correlation (H is real) and at every k on an odd chain, whose centre is a mirror point; on an even chain it is the usual open-chain approximation. At finite temperature both signs of omega carry weight, related by detailed balance, S(k, -omega) = e^{-beta omega} S(k, omega), up to the window’s broadening.

Parameters:

  • n_omega (int) – Points of the omega grid on [-omega_max, omega_max].

  • omega_max (float, optional) – Default pi / dt, the Nyquist frequency of the time grid.

  • eta (float, optional) – Window width; default 3 / t_max.

Returns:

  • ks (np.ndarray, shape (L,)) – Momenta 2 pi m / L.

  • omegas (np.ndarray, shape (n_omega,))

  • S (np.ndarray, shape (L, n_omega), real)

conductivity(*, n_omega=401, omega_max=None, eta=None)[source]¶

Re sigma(omega, T), the optical conductivity per site, for omega >= 0.

Re sigma(omega) = (1 - e^{-beta omega}) / (2 L omega) int dt e^{i omega t} C(t), whose omega -> 0 limit is beta / (2 L) times the integral. The Fourier transform is the one of ThermalStructureFactor.spectrum(). Re sigma is even in omega, and only omega >= 0 is computed: below zero the Kubo factor grows as e^{beta |omega|}, against a windowed transform that is not exponentially small.

Parameters:

  • n_omega (int) – Points of the omega grid on [0, omega_max].

  • omega_max (float, optional) – Default pi / dt, the Nyquist frequency of the time grid.

  • eta (float, optional) – Window width; default 3 / t_max.

Returns:

  • omegas (np.ndarray, shape (n_omega,))

  • sigma (np.ndarray, shape (n_omega,))

Energy, local observables, correlations and susceptibility from beta = 0 to beta.

Parameters:

  • beta (float) – The largest inverse temperature of the scan.

  • dbeta (float) – The cooling step. beta / dbeta is rounded up to a whole number of steps, so the step shrinks to end exactly at beta. The error is second order in it.

  • max_bond (int) – Bond dimension cap of the purified state.

  • cutoff (float) – SVD truncation weight cutoff.

  • susceptibility (bool) – Also compute the susceptibility, at the cost of L overlaps per measurement.

  • engine (quantx_engine.Engine, optional)
