quantx_engine.contrib.mclachlan¶

M122: McLachlan variational quantum dynamics.

Implements time evolution of parametrised quantum states via the McLachlan variational principle (MVP), which minimises

‖(iδ/δt - H)|ψ(θ)⟩‖²

over the velocity dθ/dt of the variational parameters. This yields the linear system

M(θ) θ̇ = V(θ)

where M_ij = Re⟨∂_i ψ|∂_j ψ⟩ is the quantum geometric tensor (real part) and V_i = Re⟨∂_i ψ|(-iH)|ψ⟩ is the force vector.

Both real-time (Schrödinger) and imaginary-time evolution are supported.

Classes¶

McLachlanResult

Container for one time-evolution trajectory.

McLachlanSolver

Variational-parameter ODE solver (Euler / RK4 integrators).

References

[1]

McLachlan, A. D. (1964). A variational solution of the time-dependent Schrodinger equation. Mol. Phys. 8, 39–44. https://doi.org/10.1080/00268976400100041

[2]

Endo, S. et al. (2020). Variational quantum simulation of general processes. Phys. Rev. Lett. 125, 010501. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.125.010501

[3]

Yuan, X. et al. (2019). Theory of variational quantum simulation. Quantum 3, 191. https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2019-10-07-191

class quantx_engine.contrib.mclachlan.McLachlanSolver(ansatz, hamiltonian, n_params, dt=0.01, evolution='real', integrator='euler', reg=1e-06, eps=1e-05)[source]¶

Bases: object

Time-evolve a parametrised ansatz under the McLachlan variational principle.

Parameters:

  • ansatz (callable) – Function ansatz(params) -> state_vector that maps variational parameters to the corresponding normalised quantum state.

  • hamiltonian (array-like or callable) – Hamiltonian. Either a 2-D complex array (dense matrix) or a callable H(state) -> ndarray representing the action H|ψ⟩.

  • n_params (int) – Number of variational parameters.

  • dt (float) – Time step.

  • evolution ({"real", "imaginary"}) – “real”: unitary Schrödinger evolution under e^{-iHt}. “imaginary”: non-unitary cooling under e^{-Ht} (find ground state).

  • integrator ({"euler", "rk4"}) – ODE integrator.

  • reg (float) – Regularisation added to the diagonal of the metric tensor M to avoid singular linear systems.

  • eps (float) – Finite-difference step for computing parameter gradients ∂|ψ⟩/∂θᵢ.
